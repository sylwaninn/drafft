**Ce qui marche chez les meilleurs :** une idée par phrase, un verbe concret, une promesse vérifiable
(Hinge, Breeze), un ton qui assume un parti pris.

**Codes du secteur (à ne pas copier) :** anti-swipe, « X less, Y more », sueur, « 100 % sportifs »,
« perfect match », « small talk », « It's a match ».

**Risques FR :** « plan » (plan cul, plan d'un soir), « partenaire », « se croquer », « endurance »,
« transpirer ensemble ». Élitisme : la presse reproche aux apps sportives de juger les corps.

**Territoires libres :** le geste de proposer (c'est toi qui proposes la séance), la ligne de départ
et l'élan, le « rendez-vous sportif », le niveau comme marque de respect, la précision (jour, heure)
comme preuve d'intention.

**« Meet me on the startline » :** libre dans la rencontre. À noter : l'écho Nike Run Club, la
structure de Surf, et « startline » en un mot se lit comme une faute (on écrit « start line »).
Recherche de marque INPI/EUIPO à faire avant usage public.

## 3. Synthesis

### What works
1. **Short, parallel, rhythmic lines**: "Swipe less. Sweat more." (bpm), "No chatting, just dating" (Breeze), "Se croiser. Se rencontrer." (happn), "Designed to be deleted" (Hinge). Two beats, a contrast, no adjectives.
2. **A mechanism stated as an equation**: "Un match = un date" (Breeze). Unlike vague promises, this says exactly what happens after a match. drafft's own version would be "a match = an invitation to train".
3. **Proof of authenticity**: "people who actually train" (bpm, DateFit), "Real people, real faces, real sweat", "human-verified". Verification is now table stakes.
4. **Named, concrete scenes**: "a sunrise run, a climbing session or a padel match" (bpm), "Running au lever du jour, tennis façon Federer le dimanche" (Partners). Specific beats generic "sports".
5. **Insider jargon from each sport** (WOD, PB, date pace, open match, kudos) signals belonging, but only works within one tribe.

### Sector codes (now generic, not differentiating)
- Anti-swipe framing: "Swipe less", "No swiping", "no endless chatting", "ditch the apps". **Everyone uses it**: bpm, Breeze, Timeleft, Thursday, Surf, Once.
- "X less, Y more": bpm "Swipe less. Sweat more.", Surf "less time scrolling and more time dating", Thursday "Less matching. More meeting." This formula is used up.
- "Skip the awkward drink / plus de premiers rendez-vous gênants / pas de small talk": used by bpm, Breeze and Timeleft.
- "100% sportifs / exclusivement pour": bpm, FitVybe, Woddy.
- "Real / authentique / vraies connexions": everywhere.
- FR: **tu** is the norm for dating apps (Tinder, Bumble, happn store, Breeze, Timeleft, bpm, Leets, Partners). **vous** marks Hinge FR, Once, PuLs, older sites and Playtomic. drafft should use **tu**, since that matches the category and its audience. **vous** would place it alongside Hinge/Once (premium but distant), or alongside the old SEO sites.
- FR anglicisms are accepted: "un date", "matcher", "crush", "run", "small talk".

### Clichés to avoid
- "Find your perfect match" (Fitafy, Zeal, Once US, Playtomic), "soulmate" / "swole mate" / "swolemate", "gym crush", "sweat together" / "couples who sweat together stay together" / "People who train together, stay together" (DateFit), "swipe right", "spark", "chemistry", "find your person/people" (Bumble, BFF, Meetup), "catching feels", "love at first sight".
- bpm now owns "Swipe less. Sweat more.", "Sweat first, talk later", "Your next match is already training", "Date through sport / Date au sport" and "Zero catfish / Zéro fake". Stay away from anything built on "sweat" or "sueur".
- "people who actually train": bpm and DateFit both use it.
- The FR opener "Marre de… ?": bpm, Leets and Partners all use it.

### Wording risks
- **FR "plan"**: slang for a set-up or opportunity, and "**plan cul**" means a "rencontre programmée dans le but d'avoir des relations sexuelles, généralement sans suite" (Wiktionnaire). "Un bon plan" can mean the person themselves. So avoid "planifie ton date", "un plan sport", "t'as un plan ce soir ?" and "plans" as a noun in FR UI. Thursday's "Just plans." would be risky in French. Safer verbs and nouns: *proposer une séance*, *fixer un rendez-vous*, *caler une séance*, *donner rendez-vous*.
- **"Se croquer", "craquer", "sueur/transpirer ensemble", "corps", "performance", "endurance", "cardio", "partenaire"**: all can read as sexual in FR. "Partenaire" is a sport word but in dating it's close to "partenaire sexuel". Prefer *binôme* or *coéquipier·e*, or be explicit ("partenaire d'entraînement").
- **EN**: "hook up", "score", "stamina", "workout together tonight", "come train with me" at night, "spot me". Keep invitations daytime and venue-specific. "Session" is neutral in EN and FR (*séance*).
- **"Match"** has a double meaning in tennis and padel (Playtomic "perfect match"). That can be a deliberate pun, but it adds to the cliché.
- **"Rendez-vous"** means date in FR but a meeting in EN. It's safe in FR and a bit precious in EN.
- **Elitism risk** (Dazed critique): "athletes only", "100% sportifs" and "people who actually train" can come across as gatekeeping or body-judging. Use level ("level", *niveau*) as an inclusive detail, not a filter for status.
- **Trademark and echo risks**: Tinder "It Starts With a Swipe™"; Nike NRC's "See you on your next starting line"; Surf's "Meet Me On The Grid".

### Free territories (no one owns them clearly)
1. **The match as an invitation / the proposal as the first move.** Nobody frames the core action as *proposing* a session with sport, place, time and level. bpm says the date *is* a session. Breeze says the app plans the date. **drafft can own the user's act of inviting**: "Propose a session", "Your move: sport, spot, time", "Invite, don't chat."
2. **The start line and start signal (départ, "à vos marques", "top départ", "prêt·e ?", "le coup d'envoi").** Nobody in dating owns the moment *before* the effort: nerves, the warm-up, lining up side by side. The only FR precedent is a dated site's body copy ("ligne de départ"). NRC owns the emotional sign-off "next starting line" in coaching, not in dating. Surf owns "Meet Me On The ___" in dating.
3. **Élan.** No dating app found using it (search found none). It means momentum, impulse and romantic inclination all at once, which fits the FR market.
4. **Rendez-vous sportif / rendez-vous d'entraînement.** Used only descriptively (SEO pages). Nobody has made it a brand phrase.
5. **Shared rhythm / pace / cadence** (*même allure, même rythme*). bpm is on "rythme" (its store copy, and "bpm" itself means a heart-rate rhythm), and Tinder did "Date Pace" in the UK. Partly taken, so treat with care.
6. **Level as respect** (*ton niveau, pas de pression*). Nobody makes level-matching a promise of kindness. It also answers the elitism critique.
7. **Time and place as intent** ("Sunday 9am, Parc de la Villette, 10k easy"). Specific logistics signal seriousness better than any "authentic" adjective. Nobody frames logistics as a sign of intent.

### "Meet me on the startline": availability
- Exact phrase **"Meet me on the startline"**: no hits (web search, 2026-09-29).
- **"Meet me at/on the start line"** and **"Meet me at the starting line"**: no brand use found.
- **"See you at the start line"** in a dating context: no hits.
- Close variants already in use:
  - **Nike Run Club / Coach Bennett: "See you on your next starting line." / "until that next starting line"**. This is a well-known catchphrase in running culture. Not a dating use, but runners will hear the echo.
  - **Surf Dating: store subtitle "Meet Me On The Grid"**. Same syntactic frame, in dating, US market.
